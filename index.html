Subject Topic Risk Factors, Protective Factors, and Prevention

Core Framework: Biopsychosocial Risk & Protective Factor Analysis

Health outcomes and vulnerability to addictive behaviors or high-risk choices are shaped by dynamic interactions between Risk Factors (conditions increasing vulnerability) and Protective Factors (buffering conditions promoting resilience). Building strong refusal skills, healthy routines, and robust support networks empowers adolescents to navigate peer influence effectively.

1. Categorizing Risk Factors

Biological (genetics, family medical history), Psychological (chronic stress, unresolved trauma), Social (peer pressure, isolation), and Environmental (community access, lack of resources).

Risk Literacy

2. Protective Buffers

Internal traits (high self-efficacy, emotional regulation) and external assets (supportive family, positive peer groups, school engagement) that cushion against adversity.

Buffer Index

3. Refusal & Exit Strategies

Assertive verbal statements, broken-record techniques, delay tactics, and planned exit strategies (e.g., code words with parents) to withstand direct peer pressure.

Refusal Mastery

4. Healthy Routine Architecture

Designing daily wellness routines—consistent sleep, physical exercise, creative outlets, and structured study habits—that naturally reduce stress spikes and vulnerability.

Routine Resilience

Biopsychosocial Risk & Protective Factor Mapping Matrix

Domain Risk Factor (Vulnerability) Protective Factor (Resilience)
Biological Family history of addiction, genetic predisposition. Physical wellness habits, adequate sleep hygiene.
Psychological Untreated anxiety, impulsivity, poor coping skills. High emotional regulation, problem-solving skills.
Social / Peer Peer pressure to engage in high-risk behaviors, isolation. Positive peer networks, supportive adult mentors.
Environmental Easy availability of substances, lack of community safety. Safe school climate, access to extra-curricular clubs.

Prevention Workshop & Advocacy Campaign

Complete the 5 interactive tasks below to analyze risk factors, design refusal tactics, and create a school-wide prevention message.

Task 1: Retrieval Practice

5 Min Warm-Up

Recall from Guide 5: Briefly state why addiction is classified as a biological/psychological health condition rather than a moral failure, and name one major domain of functional impairment.

Task 2: Scenario Analysis & Biopsychosocial Mapping

Factor Mapping

Scenario: "Leo moves to a new city, feels isolated, experiences high academic pressure, and has a family history of substance dependence. However, he loves basketball and has a supportive older sister."
Task: Map out Leo's Risk Factors (biological, psychological, social) and Protective Factors.

Task 3: Refusal Skills & Exit Strategy Scripting

Skill Practice

Draft 2 distinct refusal scripts to handle peer pressure at a social event: (1) An assertive direct refusal statement, and (2) A planned exit strategy using a code phrase or delay line.

Task 4: Designing Protective Routines & Support Networks

Habit Design

Detail 3 daily protective habits (e.g., exercise, mindfulness, structured sleep) and list 2 trusted school/community support resources that strengthen resilience.

Task 5: Creating a Prevention Advocacy Message

Campaign Design

Create a school-wide prevention message or slogan targeted at 11th-grade peers. Include a strong catchphrase and 2 action steps that promote resilience and support-seeking.
